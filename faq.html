Frequently Asked Questions

Answers to the questions we hear most about the South Carolina Equine Alliance.

What is the SCEA? +

The South Carolina Equine Alliance is a membership organization bringing together groups and individuals from all across South Carolina’s equine industry to support, promote and grow the industry together. This is an effort to unite trainers, jockeys, track owners, farmers, small business owners, polo players, fox hunters…you name them behind one common organization that can collectively help advance the interests of South Carolina’s horse industry with one strong voice.

Who are the members? +

We have members from all across the equine industry. And we are actively recruiting new members and encouraging any business or individual who works with the horse industry, or benefits from it, to visit our website and sign up today! Our board consists of Gordon Lawson with the Aiken Horse Park; Travis Durr with the Travis Durr Training Center in St. Matthews; and Arch Kingsley, a former champion steeplechase jockey, who is based in Camden where he runs Long Leaf Stable.

Who is funding the SCEA? +

We receive our funding from groups and individuals who support the growth of the equine industry in South Carolina.

Why was the group formed? +

The SCEA was formed because it’s past time we elevated South Carolina’s position in the equine world and worked together across the industry to do so. The first horse race took place in South Carolina in 1734—141 years prior to the first running of The Kentucky Derby! We are home to legendary locations in horse racing like Aiken and Camden. We have it all here really—from thoroughbred races, polo fields, steeplechase events, harness racing, you name it. And we have the climate and location to do even more. The growth of the equine industry here in South Carolina is not only good for those who work directly in it—it also benefits all the other businesses and industries that work with us—like our farmers, restaurants & hotel owners and employees, small businesses and etc. This group brings all these voices, and all this history, together to tell the story of the equine industry in South Carolina and to advance the industry further.

Why was it formed now? +

No time like the present. For a lot of us we’ve seen what has happened for the equine industry in Kentucky, obviously, but also recently in other southern states like Virginia for example. The industry is thriving in those places, and frankly we want South Carolina to support horses and horse related interests to a greater extent so we can grow as well. With our climate, location and history we have everything needed to make South Carolina a national leader in the equine space—uniting our voices will help make that happen.

What will the group be advocating for specifically? +

Right now we are focused on telling the story of South Carolina’s horse heritage and history, and what the equine industry is already doing to support job growth and generate tax revenue in our state. That’s our mission as we get underway—to help South Carolinians learn more about an industry that is far more than just a good time once a year at the Carolina Cup. But in the months and years ahead we will certainly be also looking for policies, proposals and ideas that can help our industry grow further. And we will be a strong voice for them when that is appropriate.

How can groups/individuals join the SCEA? +

They can visit our website and join online!
